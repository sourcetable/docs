## Hypothesis testing

### t-tests

Compare means between groups:

- "Is there a significant difference in conversion rates between the two landing pages?"
- "Run a paired t-test comparing before and after treatment scores"
- "Compare the average order value between mobile and desktop users"

```python
from scipy import stats

# Independent samples t-test
t_stat, p_value = stats.ttest_ind(group_a, group_b)

# Paired t-test
t_stat, p_value = stats.ttest_rel(before, after)

# One-sample t-test
t_stat, p_value = stats.ttest_1samp(sample, population_mean)
```

### Chi-square tests

Test relationships between categorical variables:

- "Is there a relationship between customer segment and product preference?"
- "Run a chi-square test on subscription tier vs. churn status"

### ANOVA

Compare means across multiple groups:

- "Is there a significant difference in sales performance across the four regions?"
- "Run a one-way ANOVA on test scores by teaching method"
- "Perform a two-way ANOVA on revenue by region and product category"

### Non-parametric tests

When your data doesn't meet normality assumptions:

- **Mann-Whitney U** — "Compare these two groups without assuming normal distribution"
- **Kruskal-Wallis** — "Compare satisfaction scores across five departments"
- **Wilcoxon signed-rank** — "Test if the training improved scores"
- **Kolmogorov-Smirnov** — "Is this data normally distributed?"
